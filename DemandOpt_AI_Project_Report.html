Executive Summary / Abstract

In contemporary enterprise supply chains and retail environments, inventory mismanagement represents one of the largest drains on corporate profitability. Organizations consistently battle two opposing operational risks: excess inventory (which bloats holding and capital carrying costs, induces depreciation, and limits liquidity) and insufficient inventory (which triggers stock-out catastrophes, customer attrition, and unfulfilled demand). Traditional inventory management platforms remain largely reactive, tracking stock only after transactions occur and relying on human intuition or static threshold rules.

DemandOpt AI is an intelligent, full-stack enterprise web application engineered to transform inventory control from a reactive tracking mechanism into a predictive, cost-minimizing decision engine. Built on a decoupled architecture utilizing Java 21/25, Spring Boot 3.4.3, Spring Security 6 (JJWT 0.12), Spring Data JPA, MySQL 8, React 18, and Vite, the platform incorporates:

  1. Time-Series Machine Learning Models: A hybrid forecasting ensemble combining First-Order Exponential Smoothing, Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Linear Trend Regression, and Weighted Moving Averages to project future consumption along with a 95% Confidence Interval band.
  2. Operations Research Optimization: Automated calculations of dynamic Safety Stock (SS), Reorder Points (ROP), and Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) derived from supplier lead times and historical demand volatility.
  3. Closed-Loop Replenishment Lifecycle: An end-to-end operational pipeline linking atomic point-of-sale customer order fulfillment, dynamic stock status categorization (IN_STOCK, LOW_STOCK, OUT_OF_STOCK, OVERSTOCKED), and single-click purchase order restock synchronization.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Inventory represents the physical stock of goods that an enterprise maintains to facilitate customer demand and business operations. Traditional inventory tools suffer from 7 critical bottlenecks:

  • Manual Monitoring: Periodic spreadsheet updates with severe synchronization lag.
  • Unexpected Stock-Outs: Lack of automated early warnings before stock depletes.
  • Overstocking: Capital frozen in excess stock incurring depreciation and warehouse overhead.
  • Difficult Demand Estimation: Lack of statistical algorithms to project consumer demand.
  • Intuition-Based Reorders: Order quantities determined by human guesswork.
  • Data Fragmentation: POS orders and warehouse inventories stored in disconnected silos.
  • Neglected Sales History: Historical sales data collected without predictive extraction.
Formal Problem Statement:
"To develop a web-based intelligent inventory management system that can monitor stock, maintain sales history, forecast future demand, and support inventory replenishment decisions."

3. Mathematical Models & Optimization Formulations

3.1 First-Order Exponential Smoothing (α = 0.35)

Recursively assigns exponentially decreasing weights to past consumption observations:

St = α · yt + (1 - α) · St-1

3.2 Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Linear Trend Regression

Determines demand growth momentum by finding the optimal slope (m) and intercept (c):

m = [ n ∑(t · yt) - ∑t ∑yt ] / [ n ∑t² - (∑t)² ]
c = [ ∑yt - m ∑t ] / n
Forecast: ŷn+1 = m · (n + 1) + c

3.3 Hybrid Ensemble Model

Combines models to eliminate single-model bias and variance:

Predicted Demand = (0.40 · Exponential Smoothing) + (0.35 · Linear Trend) + (0.25 · Moving Average)

3.4 95% Confidence Interval Boundaries

Lower Bound = Predicted Demand × (1 - 1.96 · StdError)
Upper Bound = Predicted Demand × (1 + 1.96 · StdError)

3.5 Safety Stock (SS) & Reorder Point (ROP)

Safety Stock (SS) = Z × σdaily × √(Lead Time)
Reorder Point (ROP) = (Daily Demand × Lead Time) + Safety Stock
(where Z = 1.65 for a 95% cycle service level, and σdaily is standard deviation of demand)

3.6 Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)

Minimizes the sum of annual order setup costs and holding costs:

EOQ = √[ (2 × Annual Demand × Order Cost) / Holding Cost per Unit ]

4. The 5 Core System Modules

  • Module 1: Authentication & Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Stateless JWT token verification, BCrypt password hashing, distinct permissions for ROLE_ADMIN (catalog modifications, batch forecasting) and ROLE_INVENTORY_MANAGER (sales transactions, manual adjustments, reorders).
  • Module 2: Product & Dynamic Inventory Management: Product parameter management (SKU, Lead Time, Order Cost, Holding Cost Rate). Dynamic stock state assignment (IN_STOCK, LOW_STOCK, OUT_OF_STOCK, OVERSTOCKED) with audit-safe adjustments (RESTOCK, RETURN, DAMAGE, INVENTORY_COUNT).
  • Module 3: Sales Processing & Transaction Engine: Multi-product point-of-sale checkout, atomic stock deduction preventing negative inventory, and continuous time-series sales generation.
  • Module 4: AI Demand Forecasting Engine: Dynamic historical time-series extraction via JPQL, multi-model execution, 95% confidence interval estimation, and automated nightly batch forecasting via Spring @Scheduled.
  • Module 5: Inventory Optimization & Replenishment (EOQ): Continuous ROP breach monitoring, automated EOQ recommendation generation, and single-click purchase order restock synchronization (PENDING → APPROVED → ORDERED).

6. Conclusion

DemandOpt AI delivers an end-to-end, cost-minimizing inventory management platform. By uniting Spring Boot 3, React 18, statistical machine learning, and Operations Research formulations, the system:

  1. Replaces manual estimation with statistical demand forecasts and 95% confidence interval boundaries.
  2. Eliminates stock-outs via dynamic Reorder Points (ROP) and calculated Safety Stock buffers.
  3. Minimizes annual inventory carrying costs using Economic Order Quantity (EOQ).
  4. Ensures enterprise data integrity and role governance via stateless JWT security.
